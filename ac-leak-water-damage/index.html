AC Leak Water Damage in Laguna Niguel: What to Know

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

The Platform and Closet Walls

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

What We Do

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Extract and Open Up

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
